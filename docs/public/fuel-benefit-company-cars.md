# Fuel benefit for company cars

> Summary of public UK guidance retrieved on 2026-10-01. Always check the source for the latest version.

## About this summary

The old GOV.UK page "Expenses and benefits: fuel for company cars" no longer exists at its original address. Its content now sits within the GOV.UK guide "Expenses and benefits: company cars". This summary uses that guide, plus two related HMRC pages: "Taxable fuel provided for company cars and vans (480: Chapter 13)", last updated 7 April 2025, and "Van benefit charge and fuel benefit charges for cars and vans for tax year 2026 to 2027".

## What employers must report

Employers usually need to report fuel provided for an employee's private journeys in a company car. This includes fuel for volunteer work.

## When fuel is exempt

You do not have to report or pay anything on fuel, including fuel for private journeys, if either:

- employees buy the fuel for their own private use; or
- you buy it and the employee pays you back during the tax year, and their payment is equal to or more than the amount you paid.

Fuel is also exempt if the car is only used for business journeys, such as travel to a temporary workplace, as long as you are sure employees are not using it for private journeys.

Charging provided for a fully electric car that you own or hire does not need to be reported. Electricity is not treated as fuel for the car fuel benefit charge, and workplace charging has been exempt since 6 April 2018.

Any car or fuel provided through a salary sacrifice arrangement must be reported, even if it would otherwise be exempt.

## What to report and pay

If fuel for private journeys is not exempt, the employer reports it to HMRC on form P11D at the end of the tax year. The employer also pays Class 1A National Insurance on the value of the fuel benefit.

For volunteer work, the employer can reimburse fuel using the advisory fuel rates, and report through a P11D or a PAYE Settlement Agreement if they pay the tax and National Insurance for the employee.

## How the fuel benefit is worked out

The car fuel benefit charge is worked out by multiplying a fixed amount, called the car fuel benefit multiplier, by the "appropriate percentage" used for the car benefit charge. The appropriate percentage depends on the car's CO2 emissions.

The car fuel benefit multiplier is:

| Tax year | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| 2026 to 2027 | £29,200 |
| 2025 to 2026 | £28,200 |
| 2024 to 2025 | £27,800 |
| 2023 to 2024 | £27,800 |

The 2026 to 2027 figures took effect from 6 April 2026. For the same year, the van fuel benefit charge is £798 and the van benefit charge is £4,170.

HMRC gives a worked example for 2021 to 2022: a petrol car with CO2 emissions of 160g/km, first registered after 6 April 2020, has an appropriate percentage of 36%. The fuel benefit charge was £24,600 x 36% = £8,856.

You can use HMRC's online calculator or your payroll software to work out the value.

## When no fuel benefit charge applies

The charge is nil if:

- fuel is made available only for business travel; or
- the employee makes good the whole cost of all fuel provided for private journeys.

From the 2017 to 2018 tax year, the employee must make good the cost by 6 July following the end of the tax year in which the private fuel was provided.

## Reductions and partial repayments

The charge is reduced in proportion if the car is unavailable for part of the year, or if free fuel stops being provided part way through the year. However, if the employee pays back only part of the cost of private fuel, there is no reduction. The full fuel benefit charge still applies.

## Cars owned by the employee

The car fuel benefit charge does not cover fuel provided for a car the employee owns or has hired themselves.
