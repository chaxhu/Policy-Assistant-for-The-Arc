# VAT on motoring expenses (VAT Notice 700/64)

> Summary of public UK guidance retrieved on 2026-10-01. Always check the source for the latest version.

## About this notice

VAT Notice 700/64 explains how VAT registered businesses should treat VAT on buying, leasing, running and selling vehicles. The page was last updated on 19 December 2024.

## What counts as a car

For VAT, a car is any motor vehicle of a kind normally used on public roads, with 3 or more wheels, that is built or adapted mainly to carry passengers, or that has roofed accommodation behind the driver's seat with side windows.

Not cars for VAT: one-person vehicles, vehicles with 12 or more seats, vehicles of 3 tonnes or more unladen, special purpose vehicles such as hearses, and vehicles with a payload of 1 tonne or more.

## Buying a car

You normally cannot reclaim the VAT when you buy a car. The main exceptions, where VAT can be reclaimed in full, are:

- cars bought as stock by a dealer or manufacturer to sell within 12 months;
- qualifying cars used mainly for taxi work, self drive hire or driving instruction;
- cars used only for business, which are not available for any private use.

Pool cars can qualify if they are kept at the main place of business, are not allocated to one person and are not kept at employees' homes.

## Leasing a car: the 50% block

If you lease a qualifying car for business, you can normally reclaim only 50% of the VAT on the lease charges. The 50% block is there to cover the expected private use. It applies to all lease charges, including optional services and excess mileage charges that form part of the leasing supply.

The block does not apply to cars used as taxis or for driving instruction, or to short term hire of 10 days or less for a specific business need that does not replace a company car.

From 1 April 2022, early termination fees are treated as extra payment for the lease and are also subject to the 50% block.

## Repairs, maintenance and accessories

VAT on repairs and maintenance can be reclaimed if the work is paid for by the business and the vehicle is used for business, even if the vehicle also has private use and even if the business does not reclaim VAT on fuel.

VAT on accessories fitted when the car is bought is blocked if the VAT on the car itself is blocked. VAT on accessories bought later can be reclaimed if the accessory is used for business.

## Road fuel used for business and private journeys

If a business pays for road fuel, there are four options:

1. Reclaim all the VAT on fuel and pay the fuel scale charge for vehicles with private use.
2. Reclaim only the VAT on business fuel, using detailed mileage records.
3. Reclaim all the VAT and charge the right amount for private fuel.
4. Reclaim no VAT on fuel at all, including fuel for business journeys.

With the mileage method, you work out the business share of the fuel cost and then apply the VAT fraction. The VAT fraction is VAT rate divided by (100 plus VAT rate). The notice gives an example of 3,165 business miles out of 4,290 total miles, with fuel costing £368, giving a business fuel cost of £271.49.

## Fuel scale charge

The fuel scale charge lets you reclaim all the VAT on fuel without splitting business and private mileage. Instead you pay a flat rate of output tax for each car, based on its CO2 emissions. CO2 figures are rounded down to the nearest multiple of 5. If you use scale charges, you must use them for all cars with fuel provided for private use.

## Fuel bought by employees and mileage allowances

A business can treat VAT on fuel bought by employees as its own input tax if it reimburses the actual cost. It must keep the VAT invoices.

If you pay a mileage allowance, you can reclaim VAT on the fuel element only. Work out the fuel element and multiply it by the VAT fraction. You must keep records of the mileage, whether journeys were business or private, the engine size, the rate paid and the VAT claimed for each employee. HMRC accepts the published advisory fuel rates for the fuel element, as well as rates from the RAC and the AA.

## Charging electric vehicles

Businesses can reclaim VAT on charging at work and at public charging points. Sole traders and partners can also reclaim VAT for charging at home if business use is recorded. If an employee charges at home, the supply is to the employee, not the employer, so the employer cannot reclaim that VAT.
